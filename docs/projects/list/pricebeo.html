About the project

PriceBeo is a mobile app that lets users scan any product barcode in-store and instantly compare prices across Ireland’s five major retailers: Tesco, Dunnes Stores, Lidl, Aldi and SuperValu. With grocery prices up 27% since 20211 and limited tools available to Irish shoppers, the app targets a clear and validated market gap.

As CTO, my role was to lead the technical vision and strategy for the platform, translate between the business team and technical advisors, and make informed architecture decisions. I contributed the analytical spine with input from the team across the sections below. The group then built that analysis into the investor-ready report and pitch deck.

1. Market context: concentrated by design

Ireland’s grocery market is worth approximately €13 billion a year and is highly concentrated. The top five retailers capture 92.3% of spend, leaving 7.7% for everyone else2. Any price comparison tool that wants to cover Irish shoppers meaningfully needs to cover these five, which is exactly what PriceBeo does.

3. The revenue model

PriceBeo runs a freemium model. The app is free to download. A €2.99 per month Premium tier unlocks basket comparison, price alerts, back-in-stock alerts and personalised savings reports. Revenue comes from three streams: Premium subscriptions, in-app advertising and retailer affiliate income. The business carries a 92% gross margin throughout the three-year plan because variable costs are limited to server, API and payment-processing fees.

5. Unit economics

The financial projections live or die on unit economics. For a freemium SaaS-style business the core question is not “how much revenue”, it is whether each acquired Premium user generates enough lifetime value to cover acquisition cost.

NoteThe two numbers that matter

Customer acquisition cost (CAC): €1.31. Calculated from total Year 1 marketing spend (€19,700) divided by acquired users (15,000 MAU).

Lifetime value (LTV): depends on retention. Industry consumer-app benchmarks suggest 12 to 24 months typical retention for a freemium utility with weekly use cases. PriceBeo’s weekly shop cadence places it at the high end.

The calculation below is an extension I built for this portfolio to stress-test the LTV assumption across three retention scenarios. The €1.31 CAC and 92% gross margin come from the original financial model; the sensitivity analysis itself was not in the pitch but is a natural follow-on to the numbers the model produces.

Code 
import pandas as pd

price_per_month  = 2.99
gross_margin     = 0.92
cac              = 1.31
retention_scenarios = {"Conservative (12 mo)": 12, "Base case (18 mo)": 18, "Optimistic (24 mo)": 24}

rows = []
for name, months_retained in retention_scenarios.items():
    ltv = price_per_month * months_retained * gross_margin
    rows.append({
        "Scenario": name,
        "Retention (months)": months_retained,
        "LTV (€)": round(ltv, 2),
        "LTV:CAC ratio": f"{ltv / cac:.1f}x",
        "Months to recover CAC": round(cac / (price_per_month * gross_margin), 2),
    })

economics = pd.DataFrame(rows)
economics
Scenario Retention (months) LTV (€) LTV:CAC ratio Months to recover CAC
0 Conservative (12 mo) 12 33.01 25.2x 0.48
1 Base case (18 mo) 18 49.51 37.8x 0.48
2 Optimistic (24 mo) 24 66.02 50.4x 0.48

A LTV:CAC ratio above 3x is considered healthy for consumer SaaS. PriceBeo’s base case sits at 38x. Even under the conservative 12-month retention scenario, the ratio is 25x. The business recovers CAC in 0.48 months, well under two weeks.

The sensitivity of the number to retention is what makes ongoing engagement metrics (weekly active ratio, shopping-basket usage, Premium feature adoption) the critical KPIs for the platform, not top-line downloads.

8. Technical architecture

As CTO my core contribution was validating that the technical model was feasible at the price point the financials required. PriceBeo runs on a stateless REST API fronting a PostgreSQL database that holds the barcode-indexed product catalogue and retailer price feeds. Scans run through Google ML Kit on-device, which avoids round-trip latency and keeps the scan-to-result time under two seconds.

Code

Key performance characteristics:

  1. Barcode detection: 1 to 2 seconds, on-device, no network dependency
  2. API round-trip: sub-second under normal load
  3. Database query: under 10 milliseconds, indexed on EAN-13
  4. Daily price refresh across all five retailers via automated ingestion
  5. Horizontally scalable, tested to 75,000 concurrent MAU without re-architecture

Reflection

PriceBeo was the most demanding and rewarding project of my degree. Working across four assignments over the full academic year, the project developed my ability to think like a founder: validating a real problem, designing a technically sound solution, building a financial model that survives scrutiny and pitching to investors under time pressure.

The most valuable lesson was learning to hold technical decisions and commercial realities in tension at the same time. As CTO I repeatedly had to choose between technically cleaner architectures and commercially sensible ones. The PostgreSQL decision is a good example: a NoSQL document store would have been faster to build, but PostgreSQL’s strict schema gave the data accuracy guarantees the consumer trust narrative depended on. That kind of trade-off does not exist in a module exercise with a single optimisation target. It only emerges when technical, financial and brand decisions sit in the same meeting.

Building the analytical sections on this page was a separate exercise from the module itself. The original pitch used static exhibits in a PowerPoint deck; reproducing the same underlying model as executable Python with Plotly for this portfolio forced me to revisit every assumption end-to-end. Several numbers I had taken for granted during the module needed to be reconstructed from the source spreadsheet, which turned into a useful audit of the financial model’s internal consistency.

The Dragons Den pitch itself was a genuine test of composure under pressure. Reconstructing the full analytical backbone as a standalone, interactive page, rather than as static PDF exhibits, has given me an output I can genuinely point recruiters at.
