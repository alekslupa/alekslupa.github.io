In one paragraph

I’m interested in the problems where a clean dataset, a clear business question and a messy real-world constraint collide. That combination is where my Allied World underwriting work, my BAA1027 Machine Learning research on shopper intent, the EcoEnergy profitability dashboard and my BAA1003 capstone on PriceBeo all sit. Business analytics as a discipline is less about running more models and more about asking the right question, building the evidence carefully, and communicating the result so that a decision can actually be made. That is the lens I want to take into a graduate role.

How I work

01
Question before model
Every analytical project starts with what decision the analysis is supposed to support. My ML research paper concluded that the classification threshold (a decision rule) mattered more than the classifier choice; that finding changed how I think about applied analytics.
02
Report the negative results
Two of the three methodological innovations in my research paper did not work. I reported both in the main narrative rather than burying them. Negative results strengthen an argument by locating the positive one more precisely.
03
Translate, do not summarise
My CTO role on PriceBeo taught me that translating between technical and commercial teams is not the same as summarising. It means restating trade-offs in the language the other side uses, so that a joint decision can be made.
04
Discipline scales
The habits that make competitive bodybuilding work (consistent inputs, honest measurement, long horizons, no shortcuts) are the same habits that make analytical work trustworthy. I try to operate the same way in both.
