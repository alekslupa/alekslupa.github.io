PriceBeo

A real-time barcode-scanning grocery price comparison app for the Irish market, developed across four assignments as part of the New Enterprise Development module (BAA1003) at DCU. Pitched in a mock Dragons Den to a panel of investors.

About the project

PriceBeo is a mobile app that lets users scan any product barcode in-store and instantly compare prices across Ireland's five major retailers: Tesco, Dunnes Stores, Lidl, Aldi, and SuperValu. With grocery prices up 27% since 2021 and limited tools available to Irish shoppers, the app targets a clear and validated market gap.

As CTO, my role was to lead the technical vision and strategy for the platform. This meant understanding how the technology worked, making informed decisions about architecture and data infrastructure, and working closely with outsourced developers and technical advisors to bring the product to life. I bridged the gap between the business team and the technical side, ensuring our solution was feasible, well-structured, and clearly communicated.

The four assignments

Assignment 1 Initial concept and idea validation BAA1003

Developed the initial PriceBeo concept, defined the problem statement around grocery price transparency in Ireland, and outlined the core app idea. Established the team structure with defined roles including CEO, COO, CFO, CMO, and CTO.

Assignment 2 Feasibility study and value proposition BAA1003

Conducted a full feasibility study covering industry attractiveness, product and technical feasibility, organisational feasibility, and financial projections. Validated the concept through an 80-person survey, 4 in-depth interviews, and consultation with Quidu, Ireland's leading grocery price comparison platform. Developed a Value Proposition Canvas, customer empathy maps, and outlined the technical architecture. Produced a full financial model including startup costs of €157,720, a 5-year plan, and breakeven analysis.

Assignment 3 Marketing campaign and video BAA1003

Developed a full marketing strategy for PriceBeo covering the 2027 to 2028 period. Designed five integrated initiatives: a Scan It and Prove It social media challenge, a quarterly Grocery Transparency Report, strategic trust and advocacy partnerships, an Ireland's Savings Movement community campaign, and a retention and lifecycle marketing strategy. Also produced a marketing video as part of the submission.

Assignment 4 Business report and Dragons Den pitch BAA1003

Produced a full investor-ready business report and pitched PriceBeo in a mock Dragons Den to a panel of lecturers acting as investors. The pitch covered the problem, solution, market opportunity, competitive landscape, financial projections, and a funding ask of €100,000 for 20% equity. Projections showed revenue growing from €99,440 in Year 1 to €938,625 by Year 3, with profitability from Year 2.

Financial projections

The chart below visualises PriceBeo’s three-year financial model, comparing projected revenue against startup and operating costs across the growth period.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

years = ["Year 1", "Year 2", "Year 3"]
revenue = [99_440, 312_500, 938_625]
startup = [157_720, 0, 0]
operating = [60_000, 98_000, 210_000]

x = np.arange(len(years))
width = 0.28

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))
b1 = ax.bar(x - width, revenue, width, label="Revenue (€)", color="#4a90d9", alpha=0.9)
b2 = ax.bar(x, startup, width, label="Startup Costs (€)", color="#e67e22", alpha=0.9)
b3 = ax.bar(x + width, operating, width, label="Operating Costs (€)", color="#e74c3c", alpha=0.9)

ax.set_xticks(x)
ax.set_xticklabels(years)
ax.set_ylabel("Amount (€)")
ax.set_title("PriceBeo — 3-Year Financial Projections", fontweight="bold")
ax.legend()
ax.yaxis.set_major_formatter(plt.FuncFormatter(lambda v, _: f"€{int(v):,}"))
ax.spines["top"].set_visible(False)
ax.spines["right"].set_visible(False)

for bar in [*b1, *b2, *b3]:
    h = bar.get_height()
    if h > 0:
        ax.text(bar.get_x() + bar.get_width() / 2, h + 4000,
                f"€{int(h):,}", ha="center", va="bottom", fontsize=7.5)

plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
